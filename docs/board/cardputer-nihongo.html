旅のことば帳Cardputer Nihongo — working title

Your phone translates. The Cardputer takes the other job: it catches the Japanese you meet and makes you type it back until it stays.

All your choices are in and filled in below: eighteen ideas, the notebook look with three more as themes, pitch as a line with a hook, and a generic build that is not tuned to any one trip. Change anything here and copy the text at the bottom if you change your mind.

One day, three stops

  1. 朝 Morning

    5 minutes · no connection

    Yesterday's caught words and today's pack come back as typed cards.

  2. 昼 Out and about

    30 to 90 seconds at a time · no connection

    Catch a word. Rehearse before you walk in. One round of prices while the train comes.

  3. 夜 Evening

    10 minutes · online through your phone

    Three sentences about your day, corrected by Claude. Today's words become tomorrow's cards, with their sound.

The evening feeds the next morning, so the device always drills words you met yourself. It works without a connection all day and goes online once, in the evening, through your phone.

Pronunciation, in three layers

Written and heard, both. The written line works in silence on a train. Sound comes from clips prepared in advance. The microphone lets you hear yourself next to the model. The device cannot judge your pronunciation by itself, and says so.

  1. 読む Written

    Always, in silence

    Every word and phrase carries a pronunciation line: kana split into beats, a line showing where the voice is high and where it falls, dots under whispered vowels, and romaji if you want it.

  2. 聞く Heard

    Speaker, or wired earphones on an ADV

    Natural-speed and slow clips for every item, made on a computer in advance. A word you catch on the road shows its written line at once and gets its clip at the evening sync.

  3. 言う Spoken

    Hotel room or a quiet street

    The device records you and plays model, you, model. In the evening a cloud service can score your sounds and fluency. No service scores pitch, so for pitch your own ear is the judge.

How should pitch be written?

The same four words in three notations. 箸, 橋 and 端 are all はし; only the pitch tells them apart.

Your four looks, on three screens each

Home screen, word catcher and sign drill. All four ship as themes, with a key to switch. The notebook is the one the device starts in.

駅 Station sign

Dark field with white plates for the header and the line you type. The sign drill looks like a station name plate.

手帳 Pocket notebook

Ruled page. A highlighter marks the chosen entry and every saved word gets a red stamp.

ゲーム Game windows

Two windows. The daruma speaks in the lower one, so every result reads as a line of dialogue.

和紙 Paper and vermilion

Light and quiet, with the largest kanji of the four.

How will it get online?

It needs 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Hotel networks with a login page will not work, so the phone is the dependable route.

What the hardware can and cannot do

Researched from primary sources. A second, independent check of every line is still running; I will correct this table if it finds anything.

TopicFactWhat it means for the design
Screen1.14 inch, 240 × 135 pixels, about 25 × 14 mm.15 × 8 Japanese characters at 16 px, or 20 × 11 at 12 px. One thought per screen, with the reading on its own line.
Japanese fontsBuilt into the graphics library. The full set of 9,500 kanji costs 301 KB at 12 px, 456 KB at 16 px and 726 KB at 24 px. The smaller set lacks menu kanji such as 餃 and 鮪.Three sizes of the full set fit in the 8 MB of storage. I measured these figures from the library's source.
MemoryEvery model has an ESP32-S3 with 8 MB of flash and no extra memory chip. About 300 KB of working memory is free, less with Wi-Fi on.The dictionary, audio and cards live on the microSD card behind indexes built on the Mac. No speech recognition and no AI model run on the device.
SoundA small speaker on every model. A headphone socket on the ADV only. Bluetooth earbuds cannot work, because the chip has Bluetooth LE only.With an ADV and wired earphones, listening drills work on a platform. Otherwise they belong in the hotel room.
MicrophonePresent on every model. With the standard library, microphone and speaker take turns. On the ADV some toolchain versions are reported to record silence.Record, then play back. The hardware check tests the microphone first, before anything is built on it.
ClockNo clock chip. The date is lost when the device is switched off.Review scheduling takes the date from Wi-Fi when there is a connection and counts sessions when there is none.
Internet2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only. It joins networks with an ordinary password; networks with a login page do not work. An iPhone hotspot needs Maximise Compatibility switched on.The phone hotspot is the predictable route, used once a day in the evening.
Radio rules in JapanThe Japanese regulator's database lists certifications for the Cardputer ADV (219-259647) and the Cardputer (219-249271). The mark is printed on the sticker of the small core module.Look for the mark on that sticker once. The firmware stays on ordinary Wi-Fi, channels 1 to 13, with transmit power turned down to the certified level.
Battery1,750 mAh in the ADV, 1,520 mAh in the others. Reports suggest six to eight hours of use. It charges only with the power switch ON, and slowly.Wi-Fi stays off by default, progress is saved after every card, and charging is an overnight job.
FlyingThe battery holds about 6.5 Wh, far below the 100 Wh limit for hand luggage. Under Japanese rules in force since April 2026, a power bank may not be used to charge a device during the flight.Carry it in hand luggage and charge it before boarding.
PronunciationClips can be generated with the pitch accent set explicitly. One cloud service scores Japanese sounds and fluency from a recording. None scores pitch.Accent is fixed and checked when the content is built. Pitch practice relies on the written line and your own ear.
No cameraNone of the models has one.You can look up only what you can type. For a menu full of unknown kanji the phone camera stays the right tool.
Updates on the roadA launcher firmware can install a new version from the microSD card or from a phone's browser.A fix on the road needs no laptop.
Existing projectsFlashcard, dictionary, Japanese input and Claude chat firmwares exist for the Cardputer. None combines a Japanese-English dictionary, typed spaced repetition and English explanations.A fresh app that borrows proven patterns from the openly licensed ones.
